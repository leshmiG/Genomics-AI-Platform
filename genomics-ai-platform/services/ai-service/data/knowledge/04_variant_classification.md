# Variant classification (ACMG/AMP overview)

## Five tiers
The ACMG/AMP framework classifies germline variants into five categories: pathogenic, likely pathogenic, variant of uncertain significance (VUS), likely benign and benign. Roughly, likely pathogenic means at least 90 percent confidence that the variant causes disease. A VUS means the evidence is insufficient or conflicting, and a VUS must not be used to make clinical decisions on its own.

## Types of evidence
Evidence is combined from several families: population data (a variant that is common in healthy people is unlikely to cause a rare disease), computational predictions, functional studies, segregation within families, de novo occurrence, and allelic data. Each criterion has a strength such as very strong, strong, moderate, supporting, or stand-alone. Combining rules then produce the final category. Newer Bayesian points-based versions of the framework exist.

## Pharmacogenomic variants versus disease variants
Pharmacogenomic variants are interpreted for their effect on drug response, not for disease causation, and a common PGx variant can be classified as benign for disease while still being clinically actionable for drugs. CPIC levels and PharmGKB evidence levels, not ACMG tiers, describe PGx evidence.

## Secondary findings
When exome or genome sequencing is done for another reason, laboratories may report secondary findings in a defined list of genes such as BRCA1 and BRCA2. Policies on whether and how to return them depend on consent and local regulation.

## Reanalysis
Classifications change as evidence accumulates. A VUS can be reclassified years later, so systems should store the classification date, the evidence version and the interpretation source, and should support reanalysis and re-contacting clinicians.
