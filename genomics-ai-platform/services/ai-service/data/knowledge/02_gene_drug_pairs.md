# Gene-drug pairs supported in this demo

This summary is a paraphrased teaching aid, not a clinical guideline. Always confirm against the current CPIC or DPWG guideline.

## CYP2C19 and clopidogrel
Clopidogrel is a prodrug that CYP2C19 converts into its active form. People with no-function alleles such as CYP2C19 *2 or *3 form less active metabolite, so platelet inhibition is weaker and the risk of cardiovascular events after a stent can be higher. Intermediate and poor metabolizers are candidates for an alternative antiplatelet drug when one is appropriate and not contraindicated. The *17 allele increases function; rapid and ultrarapid metabolizers are usually treated with standard dosing. Co-medications such as some proton pump inhibitors, notably omeprazole, can inhibit CYP2C19 and reduce clopidogrel activation.

## SLCO1B1 and simvastatin
SLCO1B1 encodes a liver uptake transporter. The *5 allele (rs4149056) reduces transporter function, which raises blood levels of simvastatin acid and increases the risk of statin-associated muscle symptoms such as myopathy. Decreased or poor function patients are often given a lower dose or a different statin, with monitoring for muscle symptoms.

## TPMT and azathioprine
TPMT inactivates thiopurine drugs such as azathioprine and mercaptopurine. Low TPMT activity lets toxic metabolites build up and can cause severe bone marrow suppression. Intermediate metabolizers may need a reduced starting dose, and poor metabolizers need an alternative drug or a drastically reduced dose under specialist supervision. Enzyme activity testing is sometimes used alongside genotyping.

## DPYD and fluorouracil
DPYD encodes dihydropyrimidine dehydrogenase, the main enzyme that breaks down fluoropyrimidines such as fluorouracil and capecitabine. Loss-of-function variants such as DPYD *2A (rs3918290) can cause severe or fatal toxicity at standard doses. Intermediate metabolizers usually start at a reduced dose, and poor metabolizers should generally avoid these drugs. Testing before treatment is recommended in many jurisdictions.

## CYP2C9 and warfarin
CYP2C9 clears the more potent S-warfarin. Reduced-function alleles *2 and *3 lower the dose requirement and increase bleeding risk during initiation. Dosing algorithms combine CYP2C9 and VKORC1 genotype with age, weight, and other clinical factors, and INR monitoring remains essential.
